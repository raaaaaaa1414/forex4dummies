1FoundationsWhat forex is, who trades it, when, and how margin works›

What forex is

The global market for exchanging currencies. It has no single exchange; banks and brokers trade electronically around the world, which is why it runs 24 hours on weekdays.

How a trade works

Currencies trade in pairs. The first is the base, the second the quote. Buying EUR/USD means buying euros and paying in dollars at the same time.

When you can trade

From Monday morning in Asia to Friday evening in New York. Activity rises and falls with the Tokyo, London and New York sessions (see the live timeline below).

Who trades

Central banks, big commercial banks, companies paying for imports, investment funds, and retail traders like you, who are the smallest players.

Why people trade it

It's open nearly all week, the major pairs are easy to buy and sell, and accounts can start small. The same leverage that makes small accounts possible also makes them easy to lose.

Margin

Margin is the deposit your broker holds to keep a leveraged trade open. If losses eat into it too far, you get a margin call, and then your trades are closed automatically (a stop out).

2Brokers, analysis and chart typesHow brokers work, the three kinds of analysis, and reading a chart›

Broker types

Some brokers take the other side of your trade (market makers). Others pass your order to the wider market (often called ECN or STP). Both can be fine; what matters is how they're regulated and what they charge.

What to check in a broker

Who regulates it, typical spreads, overnight fees (swaps), how withdrawals work, and whether there's a free demo. In the Philippines, also remember the SEC doesn't register forex brokers.

Technical analysis

Studying price charts to find patterns and levels. Most of this course is technical.

Fundamental analysis

Studying the economy behind a currency: interest rates, inflation, jobs and growth.

Sentiment analysis

Judging whether most traders are leaning one way, and whether the crowd is likely to be wrong.

Using all three

Many traders use fundamentals for direction, technicals for timing, and sentiment as a warning sign.

3Core technical toolsSupport and resistance, candlestick patterns, Fibonacci, moving averages, popular indicators›

Support and resistance

Zones where price has turned before. Draw them through areas, not single points, and trust levels touched several times more than levels touched once.

Moving averages

A simple moving average (SMA) weights every period equally. An exponential one (EMA) weights recent prices more, so it reacts faster but gives more false signals.

Candlestick patterns to recognize

A pattern means more at a support or resistance level than in the middle of nowhere. None of them work every time.

Fibonacci retracement

After a strong move, traders watch for pullbacks to 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% of that move. Pick where this pullback stops:

Bollinger Bands

A moving average with bands two standard deviations above and below. Bands widen when the market is volatile and squeeze when it's quiet, often before a big move.

MACD

Built from two exponential averages. When its line crosses its signal line, momentum may be changing.

Parabolic SAR

Dots above or below price that trail a trend. Useful for trailing a stop-loss, poor in sideways markets.

Stochastic, RSI and ADX

Stochastic and RSI measure whether a move is stretched. ADX measures how strong a trend is, not its direction.

Ichimoku Cloud

An all-in-one Japanese indicator showing trend, momentum and support zones. Busy to read, so learn it after the basics.

Keep it simple

Most indicators repeat the same information in different ways. Two or three you understand well beat ten you don't.

4Oscillators, chart patterns and pivot pointsLeading vs lagging indicators, classic patterns, daily pivot levels›

Leading vs lagging

Oscillators like RSI and Stochastic try to warn early that a move is tiring (leading). Moving averages and MACD confirm a move after it starts (lagging). Leading signals come earlier but are wrong more often.

Overbought and oversold

RSI above 70 or below 30 means the move has been fast. In a strong trend it can stay there a long time, so it's not a reversal signal by itself.

5Advanced chart readingHeikin Ashi, Elliott Wave, harmonic patterns, divergences›

Heikin Ashi

Modified candles that average recent prices. Trends show as long runs of one color, which makes them easier to see, but the prices shown aren't the real ones, so don't place orders from them.

Elliott Wave

The idea that trends move in five waves with the trend and three against it. Traders often disagree on how to count the waves, so treat it as a framework, not a forecast.

Harmonic patterns

Price shapes (Gartley, Butterfly, Bat and others) defined by exact Fibonacci ratios between swings. Precise rules, but they need patience and rarely form perfectly.

Regular divergence

Price makes a new high but RSI or MACD doesn't (or the reverse at lows). Momentum is fading, so a reversal may be near.

Hidden divergence

Price makes a higher low but the oscillator makes a lower low. It suggests the existing trend is likely to continue.

When to learn these

After you're comfortable with module 3 and 4. They add detail, not a shortcut.

6Trading the price actionPrice action, breakouts and fakeouts, market conditions, multiple timeframes›

Price action

Trading from bare price movement, levels and candles, with few or no indicators. It forces you to understand what the market is doing instead of what a line says.

Breakouts and fakeouts

A breakout leaves a range and keeps going; a fakeout pops out and snaps back. Waiting for a candle to close beyond the level, or for a retest, filters out many fakeouts (see the Breakout replay below).

Market environment

Is the market trending, ranging, or jumping around on news? Trend strategies fail in ranges and range strategies fail in trends. The ATR indicator shows how far price typically moves per period.

Multiple timeframes

Look at a higher timeframe for direction, a middle one for the setup, and a lower one for timing your entry.

DailyDirection: is EUR/USD trending up, down or sideways?
4-hourSetup: is price pulling back to support in that trend?
1-hourEntry: is there a bullish candle or break to act on?

7Fundamentals and newsMacro drivers, economic data, crosses, carry trades, news and sentiment›

Macro drivers

Interest rates matter most. Inflation, jobs and growth matter mainly because they change what central banks are expected to do next.

Data and reactions

Markets react to the surprise, not the number. If inflation comes in at 3% when 3% was expected, price may barely move.

Risk events

Elections, wars, and central banks stepping in to buy or sell their own currency can override every chart.

Currency crosses

Pairs without the US dollar, like EUR/JPY or GBP/AUD. They often have wider spreads and can move more sharply.

Trading the news

Spreads widen and price can jump past your stop at release times. Many traders simply stay out for the minutes around big releases.

Sentiment

Positioning reports (like the COT report) and broker data show which way traders lean. When almost everyone is on one side, reversals can be sharp.

Carry trade estimate

Holding a currency with a higher interest rate against a lower one can earn a small daily payment (a positive swap). A price move against you can wipe it out in minutes.

8The bigger pictureThe US Dollar Index, other markets, risk-on and risk-off, global money flows›

US Dollar Index (DXY)

Measures the dollar against a basket of major currencies, mostly the euro. If DXY is rising, most USD pairs are moving the same way, which tells you whether a move is about the dollar or the other currency.

Commodities

Some currencies follow what their country exports. The Canadian dollar tends to track oil; the Australian dollar tends to track metals like gold and iron ore.

Stocks and bonds

Rising bond yields in a country tend to support its currency. Stock markets show the general mood of investors.

Risk-on, risk-off

When investors feel confident, money flows to higher-yielding currencies. When they're scared, it flows to safe havens like the Japanese yen, Swiss franc and US dollar.

Country profiles

Know the basics for each currency you trade: its central bank, its main exports, and the data releases that move it.

Market narratives

At any time, markets focus on one or two big stories (inflation, a recession scare). The same data can move prices very differently depending on the story of the month.

Which trading style fits your life?

How much time can you watch charts on a workday?
How long would you be comfortable holding a trade?
How do you handle fast decisions under pressure?
Answer the three questions to see a suggested style.

Building a system

Define exact rules for which pairs, which timeframe, what setup, where you enter, where your stop goes and where you exit. If two people can't follow your rules and take the same trades, they aren't exact enough.

Testing it

Backtest by going back through old charts and recording every trade your rules would have taken. Then forward-test on demo. At least 50 trades of each before you trust it.

The journal

For every trade: date, pair, setup, entry, stop, target, result in R, a screenshot, and how you felt. Review it weekly to find mistakes that repeat.

Know yourself

Impatient people struggle with slow strategies; anxious people struggle with fast ones. Choose a style that suits your temperament and your schedule.

10Risk managementLeverage, position sizing, stop placement, scaling, correlations›





Leverage without blowing up
What matters is how much you risk per trade, not the leverage your broker offers. Keep total open risk small, even if the platform lets you open far more.


Position sizing
Decide your risk in dollars first, then size the trade to fit your stop distance. The position size calculator below does this for you.


Placing stops
Put stops where your idea is proven wrong: beyond a support or resistance level, or a multiple of the ATR away. Never just a round number of pips that feels comfortable.


Scaling in and out
Taking partial profit at a first target and letting the rest run, or adding to a winner. Never add to a losing trade to "average down".


Moving stops
Moving your stop to breakeven or trailing it behind price can lock in gains. Moving it further away to avoid a loss is one of the fastest ways to blow an account.


Correlations
Pairs that move together multiply your risk. Buying EUR/USD and GBP/USD at the same time is close to one big trade.

11GraduationProp firms, scams, and the most common beginner mistakes›





Prop trading firms
You pay a fee to take a trading challenge; pass and you trade the firm's money for a share of profits. Many traders fail and pay again, and some firms have shut down suddenly. Read the rules closely and treat the fee as money you may lose.


Common scams
Guaranteed returns, "account managers" who trade for you, signal groups that push a broker, fake withdrawal fees, and recruitment schemes dressed up as training.




The most common beginner mistakes




MistakeWhat to do instead


Trading real money too soonFollow the 90-day demo plan and readiness check


Risking too much per trade1–2% maximum, sized with the calculator


No stop-loss, or moving it awaySet it before entry and never widen it


Strategy hopping after a few lossesTest one system for at least 50 trades


Revenge trading after a lossA daily loss limit, then stop for the day


Overtrading out of boredomOnly trade setups written in your plan


Trading right at big newsCheck the calendar and stay out around releases


Not keeping a journalLog every trade and review weekly

Support
A price zone where buying has repeatedly stopped a fall. A floor, until it breaks.


Resistance
A zone where selling has repeatedly stopped a rise. Once broken, it often becomes support.


Trend
Higher highs and higher lows mean up. Lower highs and lower lows mean down. No pattern means a range.
